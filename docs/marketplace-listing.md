## Description

### Your Confluence content is in scope, and most of it fails

Accessibility law stopped being about public websites some time ago. ADA Title II requires
US state and local government web content to meet WCAG 2.1 level AA by 26 April 2027 for
populations of 50,000 or more, and by 26 April 2028 below that. The European Accessibility
Act has been in force since 28 June 2025. Section 508 binds US federal agencies and their
suppliers.

Public Confluence spaces, Jira Service Management help centres and Confluence-published
documentation all fall inside that scope — and what fails is almost never the template. It
is the thousands of pages your teams wrote by hand: screenshots pasted without alt text,
headings that jump from H2 to H4, tables with no header row, grey-on-white text, and links
that say “click here”.

### What this app does

**Checks each page as it is written.** An accessibility score sits in the page byline.
Open it to see every issue grouped by severity, each with the effect on a real reader and
the fix. Alt text can be applied from the panel and is written into the page as a normal
edit, attributed to you.

**Scans whole spaces.** One click walks every current page in the background. You get the
average score, how many pages have no level A or AA failure, the most common problems, and
the pages to fix first.

**Rolls up across the site.** Every scanned space feeds a single view of where the
remaining risk sits.

**Produces the artefact.** A dated conformance report, published as a Confluence page, with
findings mapped to WCAG success criteria and a plain statement of method.

**Keeps it from drifting.** Scanned spaces re-check themselves every week.

### 32 checks across 15 WCAG 2.2 success criteria

Images (1.1.1), headings and structure (1.3.1, 2.4.6), links (2.4.4, 2.4.9), tables
(1.3.1), colour contrast measured against the real background including panels and coloured
table cells (1.4.3), use of colour (1.4.1), page titles (2.4.2), embedded content and
collapsible sections (4.1.2), language of parts (3.1.2), and readability (3.1.5, 1.4.8).

The full list, with severities and criteria, is at https://clearwren.com/checks.html

### What it does not claim

No automated tool can certify accessibility. Around a third of WCAG criteria depend on
meaning, context or human judgement. This app decides what can be decided from the content,
marks the rest for review, and says which is which — in the app and in the report. It is a
way to find and fix the mechanical failures at scale, and to show your work.

### Security

Everything runs inside Atlassian Forge. Page content is read, evaluated in memory and
discarded. Only scores, issue positions and short quoted fragments are stored, in Forge
storage inside your own tenant. There are no Clearwren servers and no outbound calls.
